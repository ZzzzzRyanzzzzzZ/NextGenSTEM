The line

Five products

Sold individually because most classrooms start with one. Each card lists the questions the hardware is actually able to settle. If a question isn't on the list, the product can't answer it honestly, and we'd rather say so.

01
Helio
$95

Two 6 V panels on a protractor mount, a pyranometer-style irradiance sensor, and a switchable resistive load. Tilt, shade, series, parallel: all four are one knob away.

Questions it can settle
  1. What tilt actually maximises output, today, here?
  2. How much does one shaded cell cost the whole string?
  3. Where is the maximum power point, and does it move?
  4. Series or parallel, and for which load?

02
Gust
$180

A bench turbine with a quick-release hub, three printed blade profiles and a bag of blanks. A tachometer reads rotor speed while the load bank reads what that speed is worth.

Questions it can settle
  1. Do three blades beat five? At what wind speed?
  2. What pitch angle stalls the rotor, and how suddenly?
  3. Why does the best rotor make the worst generator?
  4. Can a blade you designed beat one we printed?

03
Cell
$210

A reversible PEM cell: put power in and get hydrogen and oxygen out, then run it backwards and get some of the power back. The gap between those two numbers is the entire lesson.

Questions it can settle
  1. How much gas per coulomb, and does theory match?
  2. What fraction of the energy comes back out?
  3. Where does the missing energy go?
  4. Can solar alone drive it, and for how long?
Consumables
Distilled water only
Gas volume
2 × 30 mL graduated
Ages
14–18, supervised

04
Store
$130

Three storage technologies on one board (supercapacitor, lithium iron phosphate, and a small sealed lead-acid) behind a coulomb counter, so they can be compared instead of described.

Questions it can settle
  1. Which one charges fastest, and which one keeps the most?
  2. What does a discharge curve actually look like?
  3. How much capacity is lost after fifty cycles?
  4. Which one would you put in a phone, and why not the others?
Cells
3 chemistries, isolated
Metering
Coulomb counter, ±1%
Ages
13–18

05
Grid
$310

The controller that turns four separate experiments into one system. Sources and loads clip onto a shared 12 V bus; the class writes the rules for what gets powered when supply falls short. It will happily let them make the wrong call and log the consequences.

Rules are written in a plain text file on the device. No app, no account, no cloud. It runs the same with the internet unplugged.

Questions it can settle
  1. Which load do you shed first when the sun goes in?
  2. How big does storage need to be to survive a cloudy hour?
  3. Is it cheaper to add a panel or add a battery?
  4. What happens if two sources disagree about voltage?

Safety, plainly

Nothing in this line touches mains voltage. The bus runs at 12 V, every battery is behind a protection circuit, the electrolyser is sealed and stores no gas, and the wind platform is rated for a desk fan rather than an open window. Full risk assessments ship with every class set and will live in the Library.
